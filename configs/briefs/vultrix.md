# Vultrix — product brief

One file per product in this folder. Alfred's marketing.draft reads it before
writing a word, and marketing.audit compares the live page against it. Edit
freely; keep facts current (especially prices) — everything he writes is
grounded here, so a stale brief produces stale copy.

## What it is
All-in-one shop management software: jobs, invoices, inventory, scheduling,
expenses, reminders, plus a built-in AI assistant. Web app, runs in any
browser — shop computer, phone, or a tablet in the bay. https://vultrix.net

## Who it is for (in priority order)
1. **Independent auto repair shops** — 1 to ~10 techs. The flagship; the
   product was built inside one. Lead with this audience always.
2. Other small businesses (invoicing, inventory, reports) — secondary.
3. Personal use (money, calendar, notes, AI assistant) — mention only when asked.

## The founder story (the hook)
Built by a working mechanic who was paying $150–400/month for slow, clunky
shop software that still could not do half of what a busy bay needs. Vultrix
is the system he wished he had on day one. Started in his own shop.

## Pricing (verify before quoting)
- Auto Repair Shop: $35/month
- Business: $25/month
- Personal: $15/month (+$10/month to add invoices & customers)
- 60-day free trial, no charge until it ends. Month-to-month, no contract,
  cancel anytime. Billing by Stripe.

## Proof points that are true today
- Built by a working mechanic, used in a real shop.
- Set up in a day; no training curve.
- Export your data anytime (CSV import/export) — no lock-in.
- Customers approve estimates and pay from their phone.
- Works on any device.

## Shop-only features worth naming
Repair orders (estimate → approved → in progress → paid), QR ticket intake
from a tech's phone with no login, VIN decode with open recalls, plate
lookup, parts tagged to the exact vehicle with one-click supplier links,
service reminders (text or email), technician hours, canned jobs, vehicle
history.

## Competitors and how to position against them
Shopmonkey, Tekmetric, AutoLeap, Mitchell 1, Shop-Ware. Position on: price
($35 vs $150–400+), no contract, no lock-in, built by a mechanic not a
SaaS company, everything in one place. Never claim feature parity we do
not have; the honest line is "everything a small shop actually uses".

## Voice
Plain, direct, mechanic-to-mechanic. Short sentences. No startup hype, no
"revolutionary", no exclamation marks. Say the price out loud. Admit what
it does not do yet.

## Channels (where the audience actually is)
Facebook groups for independent shop owners; r/MechanicAdvice and
r/Justrolledintotheshop (answer, don't advertise); iATN and Garage Journal
forums; short vertical video from the bay (TikTok, YouTube Shorts,
Instagram); parts counters and local shops in person; software directories
(Capterra, G2, GetApp); comparison pages targeting "X alternative" searches.

## Do not
- Invent testimonials, customer counts, or revenue numbers.
- Promise features that are "coming next" as if they exist (AI assistant is
  Personal-only today).
- Pitch all three audiences in one piece of copy.
